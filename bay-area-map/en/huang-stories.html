PART 01

Early life and family

From Taipei to Stanford, from lab partners to family life and careers.

01

1963 to the early 1970s

His father's refinery job took the family to Thailand. The two brothers went ahead to America.

In his Computer History Museum oral history, Jensen Huang gives Taipei as his birthplace. His father's work took the family to Thailand when Huang was a child. He and his older brother later went to the United States ahead of their parents.

At the start of the interview, Huang says he was born in Taipei on February 17, 1963, the same day as Michael Jordan. When he was about five, his family moved to Thailand, where his father was an engineer working on a refinery project. The family's roots are in Tainan. Huang himself gives Taipei as his birthplace.

Huang remembers about four years in Thailand, where the hospitality and food made a lasting impression. Political unrest in the early 1970s prompted his parents to send both sons to the United States first. The brothers spent several months with an older relative in Tacoma, Washington, then went to boarding school in Oneida, Kentucky. Huang's oral history gives no exact enrollment month.

The brothers later reunited with their parents in Tacoma. Their father found work in instrumentation engineering in Oregon, on projects such as paper mills and early chip plants. On Only in America, Huang recalls his mother doing odd jobs at a Catholic school and the family living frugally. For a trip to Disneyland, his father carpeted the back of a green car, put in milk crates for seats, and drove the family from Oregon to Los Angeles.

02

1970s

A young boarder, a teenage table tennis player, and a Denny’s dishwasher

The dorm at Oneida, table tennis tournaments in Oregon, and night shifts at a Portland-area diner are three stories Huang has told repeatedly about his youth.

Oneida Baptist Institute describes Huang as one of the youngest boarders in its history. It was a Baptist boarding school. Huang recalls having a roommate who was about 17. One taught the other to read, and the other taught him to bench-press. Huang also remembers cleaning the dorm and doing chores. The school history and his own recollections give different ages, so use “about nine or ten” in narration.

Huang attended high school in Oregon after his parents arrived in the U.S. He says he started practicing table tennis almost daily at 13 or 14 and kept it up for several years, competing in the U.S. Open and other national tournaments. Oregon State's alumni profile records a national junior ranking. In a Stanford interview, he also recalled placing third in junior doubles at 15. He joined his high school's math, science, and computer clubs, with much the same group of friends in each. His first encounter with a computer was a teletype terminal connected to a mainframe.

NVIDIA's account places Huang's first job at a Portland-area Denny’s at age 15. He recalls starting with dishes, moving to clearing tables and other chores, and eventually waiting tables. The Denny’s where NVIDIA took shape is in San Jose. The shared brand connects the scenes, and location cards distinguish the two restaurants.

In August 2019, Oneida dedicated a new building. The school says Huang and Lori provided a $2 million matching gift for Huang Hall, which includes girls' dormitories and classrooms. Pair Huang's boarding school memories with the school's ribbon-cutting footage to show his return as a donor.

03

College to around 1985

Lori Mills was his lab partner. They started doing homework together every week.

Both studied engineering at Oregon State. An introductory electrical engineering lab put them in the same group, and Huang remembers using weekend problem sets as an excuse to see her.

Huang skipped two grades in high school and entered Oregon State at 16, according to the university. He gives a straightforward reason for choosing it in his museum oral history. His friends from high school math, science, and computer clubs were going there too. His interest in math and science led him to electrical engineering.

Huang and Lori Mills were assigned to the same introductory electrical engineering lab group. In his oral history, he recalls realizing he was good at the homework, offering to show her his, and suggesting they work through problems together each week. He later joked that this was how he figured out how to ask a girl out. His recollections give different class sizes. The consistent details are the lab where they met and their weekends doing homework.

Huang earned his bachelor's degree in electrical engineering in 1984. On Only in America, he says Lori graduated roughly a year after he joined AMD and that they married soon afterward. A 1985 wedding fits that timeline. Lori is an engineering graduate in her own right, and the university's alumni records and the couple's joint gifts document her public work.

04

1984–1993

Designing chips by day, earning a Stanford degree along the way

After Oregon State, Huang designed microprocessors at AMD and systems-on-chip at LSI Logic while pursuing a master's in electrical engineering.

Several semiconductor companies offered Huang work when he finished college. He says he chose AMD over LSI Logic because it offered hands-on microprocessor design and helped pay for employees to study at Stanford. He describes the work as bridging an era of hand drafting and the arrival of computer-aided chip design.

NVIDIA securities filings show Huang worked at LSI Logic from 1985 to 1993 and later led Coreware, which oversaw the company's system-on-chip strategy. He took breaks between courses in Stanford's part-time graduate electrical engineering program and completed his master's in 1992. Working in industry, he says, let him see how classroom principles translated into products.

In 1993, Huang founded NVIDIA with Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem. Their experience spanned graphics, computer systems, and chip design. In his museum oral history, Huang emphasizes a question the team faced from the start. How could generations of dedicated graphics hardware keep supporting software that already existed?

Stanford opened the Jen-Hsun Huang Engineering Center in 2010. The university records a $30 million pledge from Jensen and Lori Huang. Film the building name and Engineering Quad to establish two different periods, his part-time studies and the gift years later.

PART 02

NVIDIA’s turning points

Founding NVIDIA, the Sega lifeline, CUDA, the AI boom, and the founder’s departure as CEO.

07

1992–1993 · From engineers to founders

Why build chips specifically for video games?

The founders had to decide what a small company could build that the established chipmakers had little reason to make.

Co-founders Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem had graphics experience from Sun workstations. Huang had designed chips at AMD and LSI Logic. In a Sequoia interview, Malachowsky recalled meeting repeatedly at the Denny’s at Capitol and Berryessa. The three saw a chance to bring 3D graphics from costly workstations to home PCs. Games could attract enough people willing to pay for the hardware.

The decision developed over multiple meetings. Huang recalls that his LSI boss, Wilfred Corrigan, introduced him to Sequoia founder Don Valentine, an experienced semiconductor investor. Sequoia's retrospective describes technically experienced co-founders facing a market question. Would consumer 3D chips sell in enough volume to sustain a company? NVIDIA was formally founded on April 5, 1993.

The first bet was faster game graphics. NVIDIA kept selling parallel-processing hardware as its core customers changed from gamers to researchers who could code, then to labs training models and finally to cloud providers.

08

1995–1999 · First brush with failure and recovery

With a Sega contract in hand, Huang went in to admit the technology was wrong

The first-generation NV1 fell out of step with PC graphics standards. Sega bought the company time, and the RIVA 128 brought in revenue.

NV1 reached the market in 1995 using quadratic texture mapping. As mainstream PC development interfaces like Microsoft's Direct3D adopted polygon-based pipelines, game developers had little incentive to adapt their software for this single card. Some NV1 cards sold, but mainstream software support never followed.

In a 2023 National Taiwan University commencement speech, Huang recalled NVIDIA realizing it was on the wrong path with the technology it was building for Sega. He says he explained why Sega should stop betting on it and asked the company to honor the payment arrangement because NVIDIA might otherwise fold. This is Huang's later account. Books and interviews describe contract payments and additional equity funding differently, so keep the two amounts distinct.

The team built the RIVA 128 afresh to work with mainstream PC graphics interfaces. NVIDIA's prospectus records commercial shipments starting in August 1997. The RIVA line accounted for most revenue in 1997 and several later quarters. NVIDIA went public in 1999 and released the GeForce 256 that year. Its first takeoff was powered by PC gaming, more than two decades before ChatGPT.

09

2004–2012 · From graphics to general computing

Gaming graphics cards gave CUDA a way to reach developers

Most buyers wanted a gaming GPU. Researchers could use similar hardware to write programs for jobs that once required specialized systems.

Early general-purpose GPU computing often meant disguising data as textures to pass it through graphics interfaces. In 2004, Stanford's Ian Buck and colleagues published Brook, a more direct way to express parallel GPU computations. Buck joined NVIDIA and led work on the toolchain. The GeForce 8800 and CUDA arrived around the same time in 2006, the product of sustained engineering across the team.

Why did people underestimate it? Gaming graphics already had a market, while other software had to be rewritten. The payoff depended on whether workloads could be split across many threads, and enterprise demand and developer tools were still immature. Huang's business decision was to build support into hardware already selling in large volumes, then give developers compilers, libraries, documentation, education, and a community. NVIDIA's technical brief reports more than 70 million CUDA-capable GeForce 8 and 9 series cards shipped by May 2008. That figure counts cards shipped.

Once applications were adapted to NVIDIA's libraries, upgrading within the same hardware and software ecosystem was often easier than migrating. That gave NVIDIA an advantage. Google's TPUs, in-house accelerators, and other GPUs can still compete, especially for large customers with the resources to rewrite their software.

10

2012–2022 · Research teams become customers

AlexNet trained on two gaming cards. Four years later, NVIDIA delivered an entire DGX to a lab

The case for GPUs in deep learning started with research. Products and revenue followed.

In 2012, Alex Krizhevsky, Ilya Sutskever and Geoffrey Hinton detailed their training setup: two NVIDIA GTX 580s, each with just 3 GB of memory, so the team split the network across the two cards. The ImageNet result showed the computer vision community what large-scale training could do. The research team created AlexNet.

In 2016, Jensen Huang delivered the first DGX-1 to OpenAI. NVIDIA published a photo with Huang, Elon Musk and others. The young lab received an entire system preloaded with software and ready to train models. When ChatGPT went live in late 2022, generative models gained a mass audience after years of use mainly by researchers. Training at greater scale and serving responses online became infrastructure challenges.

NVIDIA ran into headwinds along the way. Revenue was about $27 billion in fiscal 2023, flat from the year before. The company said distributors and retailers were running down their inventories, which weighed on gaming shipments. Revenue then rose 126% to about $60.9 billion in fiscal 2024, driven primarily by data centers. Those figures show how fast the business changed and how large NVIDIA already was.

2023–2026 · Hopper, Blackwell and competition for supply

From graphics cards to full racks as generative AI took off

Revenue surged as NVIDIA sold high-end GPUs, memory, interconnects and software together to large customers.

Hopper entered data centers as demand for training large models rose. In March 2024, Jensen Huang unveiled Blackwell at GTC in San Jose. NVIDIA began shipping production systems in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2025. In the edit, give the unveiling and the first customer shipments separate date cards.

As demand grew, two components beyond the GPU mattered. High-bandwidth memory (HBM) moved large volumes of data to the GPU. NVLink and networking connected many GPUs into a single system. The fried chicken dinner in Santa Clara mattered because SK's memory work and NVIDIA's product designs had to come together. The photos document the meeting. Any orders agreed there are unverified.

Revenue reached about $215.9 billion in fiscal 2026, up 65% year over year. Revenue also rose year over year in the following fiscal year's second quarter, reported in August 2026. Heavy exposure to AI infrastructure brought risks around export restrictions, customer concentration, in-house chips and whether customers can recoup their investments. 12

November 2026 · As of November 14

After 33 years, Jensen Huang and NVIDIA begin separate chapters

The board confirmed the leadership change. The investigation and NVIDIA's longer-term performance are still unfolding.

On November 8, NVIDIA's board announced an independent investigation. In an email to employees that NVIDIA published, Huang acknowledged being unfaithful to his wife and said he would cooperate. The investigation was still underway as of November 14. The board said it had no reason at the time to believe that national security or NVIDIA secrets had been compromised.

According to the SEC Form 8-K, the board accepted Huang's resignation as president, CEO and director on November 11, effective immediately. The company announced it on November 12. Chief financial officer Colette Kress became interim CEO while retaining her CFO role, and the board began a search for a permanent successor. For footage after this point, use NVIDIA co-founder and former CEO in the lower third.

This marks a turning point in the founder's era. To assess NVIDIA's trajectory after November 14, follow its financial results, whether customers stay and what the board's investigation finds. The CEO transition has a confirmed date. Changes in the company's competitive position take time to establish.

Late 1970s · 2021–2024

A dishwasher, a leather jacket and a packed arena

Jensen Huang often tells his story through restaurants and kitchens. NVIDIA brought those familiar settings into its product launches before putting Huang in front of a full arena.

Huang says he got his first job at 15 at a Denny’s in Portland, Oregon, where he washed dishes, cleared tables and served food. Years later, the three NVIDIA founders met to talk about a business idea at a different Denny’s in San Jose. The Portland restaurant was where he first worked. The San Jose booth became part of the company's founding story. In 2023, Huang returned to the San Jose branch to retell the story publicly with then-Denny’s CEO Kelli Valade.

The 2021 GTC keynote opened in what looked like a home kitchen. NVIDIA's production team later explained that the kitchen was a virtual set. They filmed Huang in person in a conference room at the Silicon Valley headquarters. The camera moved from the kitchen into a reconstruction of the Endeavor atrium, and the talk also featured a toy version of Huang that could answer questions. The behind-the-scenes footage shows how deliberately the launch was made to feel intimate.

In March 2024, Huang wore his leather jacket to launch Blackwell at SAP Center in San Jose. At SIGGRAPH in Denver that July, he and Mark Zuckerberg swapped jackets after talking about Llama and open models. The jacket was a personal signature that became a prop on a shared stage. Use audience reactions to the hardware announcement and the jacket swap to link the two scenes.

16

2022–2026

When Huang talks about openness, what has NVIDIA actually opened up?

Linux drivers, the CUDA Toolkit, model weights and community governance each come with different terms. NVIDIA has made different choices in each area.

In 2022, NVIDIA released Linux GPU kernel modules under dual GPL and MIT licenses. By 2024, its R560 driver recommended the open modules. New platforms such as Blackwell required them, while some older platforms still relied on proprietary drivers. This change applied to the Linux kernel modules. NVIDIA also publishes source for some lower-level CUDA libraries, including CCCL, while the CUDA Toolkit itself remains governed by NVIDIA's license terms.

At SIGGRAPH 2024 in Denver, Huang and Zuckerberg discussed Meta's open model strategy. In 2025, DeepSeek released R1 and listed an MIT license for its code and weights in the official repository. NVIDIA added R1 to its inference services and released the Nemotron 3 open model family later that year. Open models can let researchers and developers run and adapt them. Check each release for its terms on training data, weights and commercial use.

On November 10, 2026, NVIDIA announced it would contribute OpenShell to the Cloud Native Computing Foundation as a Sandbox project. It also announced participation in the Google-led Agent Substrate project. These decisions concern who governs the projects as well as who can access the code. Show the releases side by side so viewers can see where each form of openness applies.
