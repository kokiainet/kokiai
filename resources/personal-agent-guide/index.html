Three kinds of AI, three different jobs

The word agent covers three very different products. Pick by the job, not the label.

Type 1

Chat (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini)

You ask, it answers with text. Good for advice, wording and comparisons. But you still copy the text into files, open the websites, send the messages and follow up yourself. The AI stays an outside adviser.

Type 2

Work Agents (Claude Code, Codex)

The assistant moves into your working environment and edits real files: code in a project, a document in a local folder, slides, data analysis, even a website. You review finished work instead of pasting paragraphs. Most run on your own computer, so the computer has to be on.

Type 3

Personal Agents (Muse, Grok Bot, Instinct)

A secretary you reach from your phone. It has its own cloud computer (some also connect to yours), watches your email and calendar once you connect them, and brings things to you before you ask: tomorrow's seminar with the confirmation, location and what to prepare. Check-ins, meeting notes, receipts, bills, insurance, tailored job applications. When it gets stuck on a login or verification, you take over its computer, finish that step, and hand it back.

The ranking, after a month of daily use

Five products, ranked S to D. My own daily use, nobody paid for a placement.

S

Muse

Totally free. Over a month of daily use with no capacity limit and no interruption. It has handled jobs, event tickets, shopping, insurance, the calendar and this website. A successful friend referral earns a billion bonus tokens. When it hits a verification, you take over its computer and finish the step. Power tip: install Codex CLI on its computer so harder coding goes to OpenAI's advanced models.

A

Grok Bot

Separate bots for money, health and work, so topics stay organized. Included in the X plan, and complex tasks can route to stronger models. Shortcoming: a usage cap, when it runs out you wait for the reset.

B

Instinct

Free, and you text it through iMessage or WhatsApp, no new interface to learn. Shortcoming: everything piles into one long conversation, annoying when you need to find something later.

C

dots

ChatGPT's agent: it knows your history and connects to Codex. But personal plans start at $100 a month, a lot for a normal person, and everything sits in one main conversation, so separating topics and finding old material is a pain.

D

Claude Duet

Still on the waitlist, so untested. You cannot win the personal AI race if your users are still waiting at the door. Koki now has invites to share, see below.
